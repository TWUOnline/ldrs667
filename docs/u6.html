Overview

In Unit 6, our final unit, we will focus on synthesizing your professional experience in this practicum, completing the reflective practice cycle by reflecting on your learning throughout the practicum. By now, in this practicum, you have completed a minimum of 10 hours of classroom observation, designed three lesson plans, and facilitated three lessons. Teaching and facilitating is a deeply personal endeavor, and through this practicum you have challenged yourself to experiment with new facilitation skills, bravely taken on the role of facilitator, and pushed yourself to try new ways of creating authentic and meaningful learning experiences for students. As you prepare for this final reflection on your development and growth as a facilitator, you may want to take a few moments to reflect on the following questions:

  • Having completed your classroom observation, lesson plan design, and facilitation, in what ways do you feel differently about teaching or facilitating?
  • Do you think you are better prepared for a solo facilitation experience? Or perhaps more aware of how much you still need to learn?
  • In what ways have you seen the principles of communities of learning reflected in your own teaching/facilitating practice?
  • In what ways do you feel better equipped to teach in a cultural environment different from your own – or teach learners from a different culture?
  • What do you consider to be the value of integrating adult learning theory into your role as a teacher/facilitator?

Throughout this practicum, you have begun gathering materials (classroom observation notes, lesson plans, etc.) that will be included in your Practicum Portfolio. These materials will be foundational to this reflective practice experience – and to the Practicum Portfolio you will compile in this unit.

Learning Outcomes

In this unit, you will engage in a summative reflective practice of your practicum experience. By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

  • analyze your practicum experience using Gibbs’ (1998) Reflective cycle.
  • discuss how your personal philosophy of your role as teacher/facilitator influenced the learning community.
  • assess your achievement of the learning goals you set at the beginning of your practicum.

Learning Activities

  • Prepare and complete your final assignment: Reflective Practice Portfolio.

Assessment

The assessment for this unit includes your reflective practice practicum portfolio based on Gibbs’ (1998) reflective cycle. This will be your final assignment for this course.

6.1 Preparing a Reflective Practice Practicum Portfolio

Throughout your practicum experience, you will continue to engage in reflective practice. In this unit, you will engage in your final reflective practice experience in this course – preparing a portfolio that demonstrates your learning in this course. While ideally you will take the practice of professional reflection with you into your future teaching/facilitating roles, in your Practicum Portfolio, you will document your completion of the required practicum elements (Observation, Lesson Plan Design, and Teaching/Facilitation), and reflect on your learning in the practicum experience.

Summary

Completing your final assignment, the Reflective Practice Practicum Portfolio, marks the end of your journey in this course. Congratulations on reaching this milestone! I hope your practicum provided a valuable opportunity to apply your knowledge in a real-world environment and helped you realize how reflective practice can be beneficial as you seek to continually expand your expertise in teaching and facilitation. As you move forward in your career—whether as a teacher, facilitator, or in another creative role—I hope you continue to apply everything you’ve accomplished here. Well done!

Checking Your Learning
  • Synthesize adult learning theory with practical experience facilitating or teaching
  • Design authentic and inclusive learning experiences
  • Demonstrate effective teaching/facilitation skills to support critical and creative thinking
  • Integrate cross-cultural competency into the teaching/learning process
  • Apply assessment strategies to measure student learning
  • Synthesize personal identity, values, and beliefs into the facilitation and teaching process
  • Engage in reflective practice within a facilitation/teaching role
