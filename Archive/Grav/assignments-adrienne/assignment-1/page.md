## Assessment of Learning

### Reflective Practice Learning Activities

Experiential learning provides a rich opportunity for learners to integrate
course learning with engagement in facilitating learning to address
training/development needs in an organization. As part of this process, students will engage in reflective practice.

As part of reflective practice, learners will keep regular field notes of their practicum experience, including specific observations, interview notes,
descriptions of experiences, etc.

! “Field notes are the primary way of capturing the data that is collected from participant observations. Notes taken to capture this data include records of what is observed, including informal conversations with participants, records of activities and ceremonies, during which the researcher is unable to question participants about their activities, and journal notes that are kept on a daily basis” (Kawulich, 2005).

Experiential learning is strongest when it synthesizes practical experience with prior knowledge and academic literature. There is no one "correct" way to create your reflective response; rather, it is a reflection of the way you think and the manner in which you respond to your learning. You may consider traditional prose, mind map, sketchnote, audio file, video file, short slide deck, or other creative medium.

Through sharing reflections, learners will engage in reflective practice, including reflection and critical analysis of their practicum experience, organizing and integrating new knowledge with prior knowledge through regular writing practice.

Learners will complete Reflective Practice Learning Activities using a reflective practice model they select.

### Reflective Practice Learning Activity 1: Experiential Learning

Describe the context in which you will conduct your experiential learning (practicum). (Consider whether you want or need to keep the site anonymous and, if so, be sure to avoid identifying details.) List the learning outcomes you developed for your practicum experience (from your Practicum Proposal). Specifically describe how you will assess your own learning. Discuss how your practicum experience will align with your personal identity, values, and beliefs as a teacher/facilitator.

*Respond to the posts of two other learners, providing reflective feedback on their learning outcomes, practicum experience, and/or their integration of their identity, values, and beliefs with their practicum plans.*

---


### Reflective Practice Learning Activity 2: Applying a Reflective Practice Model

Outline a plan for how you will apply the key elements of Gibb’s (1988) Reflective Cycle, in your classroom observations and teaching/facilitation experience.

*Respond to the posts of two other learner’s, providing suggestions for how they might maximize their experience of the reflective cycle in their practicum.*

---

### Reflective Practice Learning Activities 3 and 4: Classroom Observation

Observe another teacher/facilitator for a minimum of 10 hours.

Write two reflections (the first after you have completed approximately half of your observation hours, and the second after you have completed most of your hours).

Using the reflective practice model you selected, analyze the lesson plan design, learning activities, and assessment strategies you observe in your classroom observation. In the analysis stage, discuss which (if any) theories of adult education and principles of a community of learning were implemented during the learning experience.

At the end of your reflection, pose an open-ended question to other practicum learners. Respond to the posts of two other learners, expanding on their experience, use of reflective practice, and/or asking a question about their experience.
