# Welcome to LDRS 667 Practicum: Personal and Professional Practice and Reflection

## Course Description
This course provides students with a practical setting in which to apply what they have learned in the Certificate in Coaching and Facilitation. Using reflective practice, learners apply learning theory, assessment theory, facilitation practices, and cross-cultural competency, in a professional learning facilitation setting. The practicum must be with a supervised business, non-profit agency, social service agency, or institution related to the student’s personal interests and future plans.

**Prior to the start of course, students will complete the following:**

1. Secure a practicum site

2. Secure a mentor at the practicum site

3. Submit a Practicum Proposal to the practicum instructor


### Learning Outcomes {-}

Upon successful completion of this course, students should be able to:

1. Synthesize adult learning theory with practical experience facilitating or teaching   
2. Design authentic and inclusive learning experiences
3. Demonstrate effective teaching/facilitation skills to support critical and creative thinking
4. Integrate cross-cultural competency into the teaching/learning process
5. Apply assessment strategies to measure student learning
6. Synthesize personal identity, values, and beliefs into the facilitation and teaching process
7. Engage in reflective practice within a facilitation/teaching role

## Practicum Overview

Students complete a site-based, experiential learning experience, integrating
academic, professional, and personal learning experiences, while meeting an
organization-based need for coaching/facilitation. Students will complete **20**
hours of experiential learning in this setting, including a minimum of 10 hours
of classroom observation and 10 hours of curriculum design and facilitation.

Through experiential learning, students engage in a rich learning experience
through which they demonstrate facilitation competencies and the application of
scholarly learning within a practical setting. “Teaching and learning can become
inherently spontaneous and student-centered when moved from the confines of the
classroom into the world at large. From the collaborative learning atmosphere
that results from the unique relationships developed outside the classroom, to
the deep learning that occurs when students must put into practice ‘in the real
world’ what they have theorized about from behind a desk, field experiences are
unmatched in their learning potential” (Claiborne, Morrell, Bandy, & Bruff,
2018).


## Assessment of Learning

Assessment of learning will include a Practicum Proposal, the design and
implementation of lesson plans, and reflective practice assignments (discussions
and final paper).

Experiential learning integrates scholarly research and prior learning with
practical experience in the practicum setting. All work submitted should reflect
scholarly thinking and reflective practice, and should incorporate scholarly
work with proper citations. APA format is required.

## Graduate Level Writing Standards

For students in 667, graduate level writing standards following APA 7 are expected. Please consult the [OWL Purdue website](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/apa_style/apa_style_introduction.html) for guidance and seek assistance from the TWU Writing Center and writing coaches as needed. Assignments have rubrics that attribute some marks to APA formatting and cannot be graded as fully meeting expectations if there are APA errors. That said, your conceptual understanding remains of primary importance. It is your responsibility to ensure polished work to the highest standard of which you are capable. This demands meticulous attention to detail, which will become more 'natural' with practice. Please seek any necessary clarification from your instructor.
